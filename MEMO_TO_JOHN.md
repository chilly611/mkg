I was framing Marketing Knowledge Garden as a fifth vertical: HKG, BKG, OKG, AKG, MKG. Same shape, same internal-first sourcing, same patience curve. Ship the graph, wait for LLMs to cite us, compound the moat over 18–36 months.

I commissioned a devil's advocate against my own plan last week. The critique landed clean: combining (no revenue) + (internal-only sources) + (citation health as primary metric) produces **no falsifying signal until late 2026**. By the time we know if it's working, we're a quarter underwater. Worse — a graph fed only on our own campaigns looks like a self-portrait. LLMs are trained to discount self-referential corpora.

That's a real problem. The fix isn't to abandon the moat play — the GEO/AEO channel is genuinely the best-leveraged new channel since paid search (Profound went $0 → $1B valuation in 24 months on the adjacent thesis). The fix is to stop pretending we're an infrastructure company at this stage. **We're a product company that happens to be building infrastructure.**

So I'm killing "MKG as fifth garden" and replacing it with a real product.

## The 30,000-ft view of AI marketing

**Where the money flows today.** The dominant revenue channels in 2026 are still lifecycle email/SMS (highest revenue-per-dollar of anything, period), paid search, LinkedIn outbound for B2B, and product-led growth. SEO still prints but is bifurcating fast as AI Overviews eat top-of-funnel queries. Most marketing dollars still chase channels that worked five years ago.

**What's new in the last 12 months.** Three things didn't exist in 2024: (1) AI agents now execute marketing work — outbound, content, creative — at 1/10th the human cost (Apollo, Clay, AirOps), (2) GEO/AEO has emerged as a real channel with real ROI ($3.71 returned per $1, 4.4× conversion vs traditional SEO), (3) agentic commerce — buyers asking ChatGPT for a vendor and getting one — is now measurable for the first time. The marketer's job is being rewritten while we watch.

**Where the white space is.** The high-leverage moves nobody's making because they require taste and timing: founder-led POV voice on a single platform (Justin Welsh built a $10M solo business this way), AI-personalized direct mail to named lists (4–9% reply rate vs 0.3% on cold email; almost nobody is doing this for B2B over $25K ACV), and vertical-specific AI marketing — picking one industry and being the only people who deeply understand how AI changes marketing *there*. That last one is where I think we live.

You sold Modio for ~$300M because credentialing was a vertical where one customer told the next, and the product was so unmistakably useful that word-of-mouth compounded faster than any paid channel could. The first three years of Modio weren't a marketing story — they were a "the Carolinas talked to Texas talked to California" story.

The Marketing Architect's first wedge should look exactly like that. Pick a niche where AI-search visibility is suddenly mission-critical, where the buyers know each other, and where nobody else is serving them with real expertise.

**My candidate: vertical-AI-marketing for healthcare.** Specifically, mid-market healthcare-tech companies — credentialing platforms, RCM tools, clinical comms, point-of-care tools — who have a sales motion that's 60% conferences and word-of-mouth and 40% inbound, and whose CMOs are panicking about being invisible in ChatGPT when a hospital admin asks "best credentialing platform." That's your network. That's a vertical with budget. And it's a vertical where HKG can act as a co-distribution channel — every healthcare CMO who buys Marketing Architect becomes a candidate to bring HKG into the same org.

I'm not saying we *only* sell to healthcare. I'm saying healthcare is the wedge, the same way credentialing was the wedge for Modio. We can fan out from there once the playbook is proven.

## The risk I'm worried about

The honest one: **service businesses are fast cash but slow moat.** If we do five teardowns and they're great, we have $10K and five happy customers but no compounding asset. The moat only forms if we are *disciplined* about every paid teardown also producing a public graph entity, every customer interview becoming structured intel, every engagement feeding the substrate that eventually becomes the software. The trap is becoming a marketing consultancy that happens to use AI. The win is being the company that turns marketing intelligence into structured, AI-citable infrastructure — and gets paid by customers to do it.

Second-order risk: 30 days is short for B2B with no existing brand. We may need 60. The version of this memo from June might say "the wedge worked but slower than Chilly thought." That's fine. What's not fine is finding out at Day 90 that nobody wants it. The whole point of the pivot is to get falsifying signal early.
