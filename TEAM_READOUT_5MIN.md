## Q&A pre-empts

**Q: Why Campaign and not Brand or Channel?**
> Channels are taxonomy — small finite list. Brands are tenants — they own campaigns. Campaign is the unit of work, the unit of spend, and the thing with a natural lifecycle (brief → creative → launch → optimize → wrap). The masterdoc rule says pick the noun the whole graph orbits. For marketing, that's Campaign.

**Q: Won't this look like just another marketing tool?**
> Tools sell features to brands. We sell structured ground truth that LLMs cite. Different category, different buyer, different moat. The competitors I named — Profound, Bluefish, AirOps — are SaaS dashboards. We're a knowledge graph.

**Q: What if a competitor open-sources their data first?**
> That's kill criterion #2 in the roadmap. If Profound or HubSpot publishes a free, public, JSON-LD'd campaign benchmark library before we hit 30 records, we re-position MKG as the cross-vertical graph — the federation moat. They can't match cross-garden links.

**Q: How does this make money?**
> The free Campaign Teardown is the gravity well. Professional subscriptions for marketers and agencies who want to publish their own teardowns and pull from the graph via MCP. Enterprise contracts for vendor intelligence and M&A diligence. Data API to AI providers. Same four-lane shape as every other garden.

**Q: When does MKG open to external customers?**
> Post-MRR. Specifically: BKG at $100K MRR plus HKG with 10 paying enterprise plus MKG citation health greater than or equal to 1 LLM cite per long-tail question. All three before opening.

**Q: What's the single biggest risk?**
> Anti-fabrication breaking under deadline pressure. Marketing is the domain most prone to LLM hallucination. A single fabricated benchmark, a single uncited metric, undermines our authority across every garden. The schema enforces source IDs; the Critic auto-escalates. But humans staying disciplined is the actual moat.
